# Aurora Robotics

Aurora Robotics is a robotics startup headquartered in Austin, Texas. The
company was founded in 2019 by **Maya Chen**, a former perception engineer at
a self-driving car company, and **Diego Ortiz**, a mechanical engineer who
previously built warehouse automation systems.

Aurora Robotics' mission is to build affordable robotic arms and mobile
platforms for small and mid-sized warehouses. The company grew out of a
university robotics lab project and has since raised two rounds of funding.

Maya Chen serves as CEO and also leads the Perception Team. Diego Ortiz
serves as CTO and oversees the Manipulation Team. Both teams are part of
Aurora Robotics' engineering organization.

Aurora Robotics develops two flagship products: the **NovaArm** robotic
manipulator and the **PathFinder** navigation stack. These products are
described in more detail in the products document.

## Origins

The idea for Aurora Robotics took shape in a university robotics lab, where
Maya Chen and Diego Ortiz were both working on separate research projects:
Maya on camera-and-lidar perception for autonomous vehicles, and Diego on
low-cost actuator design for manipulator arms. The two met through a shared
robotics reading group and realized that warehouse automation, an industry
Diego had already worked in, was a natural place to combine perception and
manipulation research into a single affordable product line.

In 2019, Maya Chen and Diego Ortiz left their respective jobs and founded
Aurora Robotics with a small team of three engineers working out of a shared
office space in Austin, Texas. The founding thesis was simple: existing
warehouse robots were priced for large enterprise operations, leaving small
and mid-sized warehouses to rely on manual picking and outdated conveyor
systems. Aurora Robotics set out to build robots that were affordable enough
for a mid-sized distribution center while still being capable enough to
handle irregular, unstructured inventory.

## Funding history

Aurora Robotics raised a seed round in late 2019 to fund early prototyping of
what would become the NovaArm manipulator. A Series A round followed in 2021,
after the company demonstrated a working NovaArm prototype picking irregular
items in a partner warehouse. That round funded the build-out of both the
Perception Team and the Manipulation Team as dedicated engineering groups,
rather than a single undifferentiated engineering team. The company has used
this funding to grow from its original three engineers to a broader
organization spanning perception, manipulation, and go-to-market functions.

## Company mission and market

Aurora Robotics' mission is to make robotic automation accessible to
warehouses that cannot justify the cost of traditional industrial robotics
installations. The company focuses specifically on small and mid-sized
warehouse operators, a segment that Maya Chen has described in interviews as
underserved by existing robotics vendors, most of which target large
distribution centers with dedicated automation budgets and integration
teams. Aurora Robotics instead positions its products as modular systems
that a warehouse operations team can deploy without a lengthy custom
integration project.

## Leadership and structure

Maya Chen serves as CEO of Aurora Robotics and also leads the Perception
Team directly, splitting her time between company leadership and technical
oversight of perception research. Diego Ortiz serves as CTO and oversees the
Manipulation Team, with a similar split between executive responsibilities
and hands-on technical direction of manipulator design. Both teams report
into the broader Aurora Robotics engineering organization, and both team
leads sit on the company's small leadership team alongside the head of
operations.

Aurora Robotics develops two flagship products, the NovaArm robotic
manipulator and the PathFinder navigation stack, which are combined in the
company's mobile picking robot. These products, along with the underlying
technologies each one relies on, are described in more detail in the
products document.
